CyberEnsō is a contemporary digital interpretation of the Zen ensō: a circle painted in one spontaneous brushstroke.

What is an ensō?

An ensō (円相, “circular form”) is a hand-drawn circle in Zen art, usually made in one uninhibited brushstroke. It is associated with the Zen mind and ideas including enlightenment, emptiness, freedom, and no-mind.

Origins and practice

The ensō belongs to Japanese Zen ink painting and calligraphy. Its movement is related to sumi-e and hitsuzendō, “the way of the brush.” An open circle can suggest movement and imperfection; a closed circle can suggest completeness. The uncorrected mark records the maker, the moment, and the conditions of its creation.

Learn more: Wikipedia's Ensō article · sumi-e · wabi-sabi.

Original project and inspiration

This project is based on and inspired by Utsob's original CyberEnsō generator. The original work connects hashing text with the destructive, one-way quality of ink and the mental clearing associated with making an ensō.

Visit the original: enso.utsob.me · Read the community discussion on Reddit.

How the hashing works

Everything runs in your browser. The editor hashes your text with BLAKE2b-256, converts the digest bytes into the original decimal digit stream, and feeds those digits into 60 layered SVG paths. The original angle formulas control the length and direction of each arc; small random variations keep repeated renders organic.

For more detail, read the project README or the architecture notes.

  • Digest: text becomes a fixed 256-bit BLAKE2b value.
  • Digit stream: the digest bytes are joined as decimal digits, with the original zero-retention rule.
  • Gesture: the first 60 digits guide arc endpoints and layered brush weight.
  • Artifact: the result is an SVG that stays crisp and can be downloaded.

Privacy by design

There is no backend. Text is never sent to an API. Saved history uses only this browser's localStorage, and you can clear it through your browser settings at any time.
