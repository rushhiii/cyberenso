CyberEnsō is a contemporary digital interpretation of the Zen ensō: a circle painted in one spontaneous brushstroke.

What is an ensō?

An ensō (円相, “circular form”) is a hand-drawn circle in Zen art, usually made in one or two uninhibited brushstrokes. It is associated with the Zen mind and ideas including enlightenment, emptiness, freedom, and no-mind.

Origins and practice

The ensō belongs to the tradition of Japanese Zen ink painting and calligraphy. Its tools and movement are closely related to sumi-e and Japanese calligraphy: an ink brush applies expressive ink to paper. The practice of drawing ensō or writing calligraphy for self-realization is often called hitsuzendō, “the way of the brush.”

An ensō may be open or closed. An open circle can suggest movement, development, and the beauty of imperfection associated with wabi-sabi; a closed circle can suggest completeness. The brushstroke is not corrected after it is made, so it records the maker, the moment, and the conditions of its creation.

Reference: Wikipedia — Ensō. This project is a digital interpretation and does not replace the traditional spiritual or artistic practice.

Original project and inspiration

CyberEnsō is based on and inspired by CyberEnsō, the Ensō Generator, created by Utsob. The original project describes hashing the text as a destructive process and an analogy for clearing the mind before painting an Ensō.

Original project: enso.utsob.me · Community reference: r/generative — An Ensō Generator

How the hashing works

The editor runs locally. Each character in your text is folded into a 32-bit FNV-1a-style hash. That number becomes the seed for a small pseudo-random generator. The generator chooses the radius, start point, and sweep of each layered arc.

  • Same input → same seed → same Ensō.
  • A changed word → a changed seed → a new gesture.
  • Thirty-four open SVG paths create the brush-like weight.
  • The SVG stays sharp at any size and can be downloaded directly.

Privacy by design

There is no backend. Text is never sent to an API. Saved history uses only this browser's localStorage, and you can clear it through your browser settings at any time.
